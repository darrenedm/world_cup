## Sub-Position Framework

| Code | Role | What matters most |
|------|------|-------------------|
| **CB** | Centre-back | Aerial duels won %, clearances/90, interceptions/90, progressive passes, duel win rate |
| **RB / LB** | Fullback | G+A, progressive carries, key passes, crosses, defensive duels won |
| **RWB / LWB** | Wingback | As fullback but weighted toward attacking output — G+A, chances created |

## Key Storylines

### Italy's Defensive Tragedy
Italy's third consecutive World Cup absence doesn't just cost them midfielders — it costs the tournament three elite defenders. **Bastoni** (Inter Milan, 27) is arguably a top-three CB in the world: 4 Serie A assists from CB, 27 Serie A apps, Scudetto winner. **Pavlović** (AC Milan, 24) scored 4 Serie A goals from CB — a remarkable output for his age. **Di Lorenzo** (Napoli, 32) is Italy's captain and most-capped defender of this generation. All three watching from home is the tournament's biggest collective defensive loss.

### Militão: Brazil's Personal Blow
**Éder Militão** represents the cruelest individual story in this list. Brazil qualified. Real Madrid wanted him fit. He returned from his ACL recovery, re-injured his hamstring (biceps femoris tendon surgery, December 2025), and is now officially ruled out with a five-month recovery timeline that makes the June 11 start impossible. Brazil confirmed it. Gabriel Magalhães absorbs the additional responsibility — he becomes Brazil's undisputed CB1 alongside Marquinhos.

### Seven Nations Fall Short
An unusual number of nations failed to qualify from this defender list: **Kosovo** (lost playoff final to Turkey 0-1), **Serbia** (3rd in group), **Hungary** (lost final qualifier to Ireland 2-3 in injury time), **Wales** (penalty shootout vs Bosnia), **Nigeria** (CAF playoff on penalties vs DR Congo), **Burkina Faso** (worst CAF group runner-up), and **Italy**. That's seven separate countries whose best defenders stay home — and several (Bastoni, Pavlović, Kerkez) are genuinely world-class.

### The Four Fitness Races
Four defenders are in significant health uncertainty heading into late May:
- **Achraf Hakimi** — hamstring tear May 8; PSG confirm season over; ~5 weeks to Morocco's June 14 opener. Partial tears carry risk of re-injury without full match sharpness
- **Joško Gvardiol** — tibial fracture surgery January 4; just returned to Man City training; 4+ months without a competitive minute. Croatia desperately need him
- **Rúben Dias** — ankle injury after earlier hamstring; near return but arriving at a WC on the back of two injuries in 4 months is a concern for Portugal's CB depth
- **Jurriën Timber** — groin injury March 2026; slower recovery than expected; Netherlands' versatility (CB or RB) means he's needed; needs a return before June to carry match sharpness

### The Grimaldo Paradox
**Alejandro Grimaldo** has the best attacking output of any fullback on this list: 8G/7A, including 4 UCL goals — numbers that would satisfy most attacking midfielders. Yet he almost certainly won't start for Spain, because **Marc Cucurella** was in the Euro 2024 Team of the Tournament and started the final. De la Fuente has shown loyalty to his tournament heroes. Grimaldo travels as one of football's most dangerous substitutes — Spain's left-back secret weapon.

### Morocco's Hakimi Problem
Morocco were the first African nation to qualify (September 2025) and Hakimi has been their defining player: 5G/10A, 95 caps, captain. His hamstring tear on May 8 — confirmed season-ending by PSG — complicates everything. The ~5-week gap before Morocco's June 14 opener means recovery is technically possible, but match sharpness is not. Morocco must decide: take the risk and use him if he clears medical, or protect a long-term asset.
